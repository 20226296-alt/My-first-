Analysis

Conclusions

Online shopping is now mainstream

80% of survey participants have made at least one online purchase. This mirrors data from the Pew Internet & American Life Project, which found that the vast majority of American adults with internet access have shopped online, a figure that has grown steadily every year since the late 1990s.

Younger adults drive frequency

The 18–25 and 26–39 age brackets together represent 75% of the sample and account for all weekly buyers. eMarketer data confirms that millennials and Gen Z are the most frequent digital shoppers, often integrating mobile commerce into daily routines.

Education and e-commerce go hand in hand

All four respondents who had not made an online purchase fell into the high-school-only education bracket. Research cited by ClickZ consistently shows that internet literacy — which correlates with educational attainment — remains a key predictor of e-commerce participation.

Amazon dominates mind-share

45% of buyers named Amazon as their primary platform. The E-Commerce Times has reported Amazon's share of US e-commerce at over 37%, making its dominance in this survey unsurprising. Its subscription model, fast delivery, and broad catalogue create strong lock-in effects.

Trust remains the #1 barrier

The three respondents who browsed but never purchased cited concerns about payment security and unfamiliar brands. Pew Internet surveys have consistently found that privacy and fraud concerns are the top reasons non-buyers avoid completing transactions.
